Edit the draft below for its intended reader and purpose. Infer the format and
audience from the supplied context. Preserve the author's position and level of
formality. A README needs a clear explanation and usable instructions. A sales
page or script may need a different opening.

PASS 0: A CHAT REPLY AROUND A DRAFT
Apply this pass only when the pattern check names chat framing: a chat opener, a
draft handover, an offer to redo or edit, notes to the requester, a remark on the
edit, an option menu, a version label or a bold subject line. The text is then
probably an assistant's chat reply. Return only what the requester asked for: the
email, post, bio, letter, README or explanation itself. A flagged line that speaks
to the text's own reader belongs to the draft: a person's email can say "Here's the
agenda:" or "Would you like me to book the room?" to its recipient. Keep such a line
as it is and say in your notes why it stays. Delete, do not rephrase, what speaks to
the requester about the draft:
- an opening addressed to the requester ("Sure!", "Great question!", "Here's a
  draft you can adapt:", "Here's a polished version:");
- version labels ("Option 1 (concise):", "Version 2:"). If the reply holds several
  versions, keep the first one in full, delete the others and name them in the
  notes;
- the "---" rule that fences the draft, and everything after it that talks to the
  requester: notes on what changed, tips, assumptions, offers ("Want me to make it
  warmer?", "I can also...", "If you share X, I can...");
- remarks about the edit inside the text ("I kept it concise", "fill in the
  bracketed parts").
If the reply shows the draft inside a blockquote or quotation marks, return the
draft without them.
Do not turn a deleted line into a new sentence about the draft, and do not change
the draft because of what a deleted line said. Move an assumption, correction or
open question it raised into your notes. Keep placeholders such as [Your Name]
exactly as they are. Keep a subject line word for word, with its label in the
draft's own language, and remove only the bold around it. That is the only bold
to remove: keep every other bold and italic in the draft as it is.
Replace a signpost heading such as "Key takeaways" or "The short version" with one
that names its content, or drop it. When you are told to return the same number
of blocks, that rule comes first: delete nothing and leave the framing in place.

PASS 1: PURPOSE AND FLOW
- Make the opening answer the reader's first question about the subject.
- Remove duplicate hooks and curiosity gaps that delay necessary information.
- Check that each sentence adds a useful point and follows from the previous one.
- Remove repeated metaphors, empty contrasts and comparisons that do not explain
  anything. Describe the relevant behavior directly.
- Explain jargon where the reader needs it. Keep conditions attached to claims.
- Keep a closing summary only if it serves this document's reader.

PASS 2: WORDING AND SENTENCE PATTERNS
- Replace vague promotional wording with direct language. Review terms such as
  "delve", "seamless", "unlock", "game-changing" and "leverage" in context.
- Remove constructions such as "not just X, but Y", "no X, no Y, just Z" and
  adjacent sentences that merely relabel the same thing for different audiences.
  Preserve a contrast when the distinction is necessary to the explanation.
- Review repeated sentence shapes across paragraphs, not just individual words.
- Replace stacked hedges with a verb that reflects the author's actual certainty.
- Review self-answering questions and decorative lists. Keep useful questions,
  lists, punctuation and technical terms when they communicate the point well.

PASS 3: CONTEXT AND ACCURACY
- Read the result as a complete document. Does it suit the reader and the format?
- Let sentence length follow the meaning. Do not force short sentences, fragments,
  contractions, opinions, deliberate mistakes or other signs of personality.
- Use concrete details already in the source where they help. Do not add a number
  to each claim or turn every document into sales copy.
- Check that the edit has not overstated capabilities, removed a qualification or
  changed the author's argument.

ABSOLUTE RULES
- Never invent a fact, statistic, quote, customer, logo, rating or result.
- If necessary information is missing, use a clear placeholder such as
  [needs number] and explain the gap in the notes. Do not guess.
- Preserve factual meaning and technical behavior. Keep how sure the author is:
  do not add a hedge, caveat or qualification the draft did not have, and do not
  weaken, replace or remove a claim or conclusion because you doubt it. Put the
  doubt in the notes instead.
- Preserve HTML tags, Markdown structure, links and placeholders exactly, except
  the chat framing PASS 0 removes. A bracketed placeholder such as [Your Name]
  stays as it is.
- Leave code blocks, commands, inline code and intentionally quoted examples
  unchanged. They may document the very patterns being discussed.
- Keep the author's register. Do not add an opinion the author did not express.
